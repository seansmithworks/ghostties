Prior art — what the field actually does
Nobody ships this. That is worth knowing before you commit, not after.
Four findings from Mobbin's pattern set, Raycast's 2026 rebuild, and the terminal-native tools that solved this problem first.
01
Mobbin studied 120+ command-palette patterns. Not one is chromeless.
Every variant they name — keyboard shortcuts, tabs, chips — assumes a card. There is no "borderless" or "minimal surface" variant in the taxonomy at all. That is not a reason to abandon the direction; it means there is no pattern to lean on and every detail is yours to invent. Worth noting too: chips are one of their three named variants, and you already built and rejected that in the Model A rebuild.
mobbin.com/glossary/command-palette
02
Raycast's 2026 rebuild went the other way — and their blur is 8× yours.
Raycast v2 redesigned for Liquid Glass: backdrop-filter blur(48px), 1px border, 8px radius. The nearest incumbent is adding chrome, not removing it. But the number is the useful part — variant 09 proposes 6px. If blur is going to carry the whole boundary job on its own, 6px is not in the right order of magnitude.
manual.raycast.com/new-in-v2 · styles.refero.design
03
Fig put its terminal overlay at the cursor, IDE-style. Not floating centre.
The one product that solved "UI over a live terminal" at scale chose adjacency — the popup tracks the cursor position and context, the way an IDE completion does. It never floats over the middle of the scrollback.
fig.io/user-manual/autocomplete
04
The terminal-native tools dodge the problem entirely: they reserve space.
vim's command line owns the last row. fzf takes a reserved region or the alternate screen. Emacs' minibuffer grows the bottom. None of them overlap live output — so none of them have a legibility problem to solve. Variant 09 floats over the centre of the scrollback, which is the one configuration none of the prior art picked. See the next board.
structural, verified against fzf.vim and vim-floaterm docs
